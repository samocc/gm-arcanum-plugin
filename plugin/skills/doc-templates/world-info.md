# World Info — Writing Standards & Template

Writing standards for `world-info.md` documents. This document defines the campaign's setting — geography, zones, points of interest, and connections. It is the GM's primary reference for grounding narration in a consistent, specific world.

`world-info.md` is the most model-driven document during campaign creation — the system proposes a full world draft from the character backstory and campaign pitch, and the player adjusts.

---

## Writing Standards

### Scope and Scale

* **Scope** should match campaign length and genre. A Long campaign in a vast wilderness needs a Continent or Region. A Short urban intrigue campaign needs a City-State. One-Shots can use a single location.
* Zone count scales to campaign length:
  * **Long (30+):** 4 zones
  * **Medium (15-25):** 3 zones
  * **Short (8-12):** 2 zones
  * **One-Shot (3-5):** 1-2 zones

### Areas or Zones

* Each zone is a distinct geographical or thematic division of the setting.
* Write each as a bullet with the zone name bolded, followed by a descriptive paragraph. Cover: what defines this zone, what makes it distinct, what kind of content happens here, relative geographic position.
* Zones should feel meaningfully different from each other — different ecology, culture, danger level, or narrative function.

### Points of Interest

* Grouped by zone. Each zone gets a subsection.
* **Named landmarks:** 3-4 per zone, each with a bolded name and descriptive paragraph covering what it is, who's there, and why it matters.
* **Other Landmarks:** A brief paragraph listing lighter mentions — waypoints, minor features, ambient locations. These provide texture without requiring full writeups. Format: "Name (one-line description), Name (one-line description)."
* Points of interest should include the campaign's starting location.

### Key Connections

* Routes, rivers, trade roads, or systems that connect zones and landmarks.
* 2-4 connections. Each is a bolded name with a descriptive paragraph covering what it is and what it connects.
* At least one connection should be the primary travel route the party will likely use.

### Writing Style

* Specific and evocative — not generic fantasy. "A massive cavern where the river slows to a near-halt, spreading into a shallow, silt-choked delta" beats "a large underground lake."
* Ground descriptions in sensory details: what you see, hear, smell, feel.
* Include faction presence, cultural details, and danger levels where relevant.
* Avoid revealing GM secrets — world-info.md is player-accessible. Hidden truths belong in gm-canon.md.

## Template

```markdown
# World Info

## Overview

* Name: [Evocative name for the setting.]
* Core Concept: [A single compelling sentence summarizing the setting's main idea.]
* Scope: [Scale of the setting. Examples: Continent, City-State, Kingdom, Archipelago, Subterranean Region, Planar Hub.]

## Areas or Zones

* **[Zone Name]:** [Descriptive paragraph — defining characteristics, ecology, culture, danger level, relative geographic position.]

* **[Zone Name]:** [Descriptive paragraph.]

## Points of Interest

### Within [Zone Name]

* **[Landmark Name]:** [Description — what it is, who's there, why it matters.]
* **[Landmark Name]:** [Description.]

Other Landmarks: [Name (one-line), Name (one-line), Name (one-line).]

### Within [Zone Name]

* **[Landmark Name]:** [Description.]
* **[Landmark Name]:** [Description.]

Other Landmarks: [Name (one-line), Name (one-line).]

## Key Connections

* **[Connection Name]:** [Description — what it is, what it connects, who controls or uses it.]
* **[Connection Name]:** [Description.]
```
