# Campaign Pitch — Template

Structural template for campaign-pitch.md documents. This document captures the player's preferences for tone, pacing, scope, and party composition. It is a foundational document — many other systems (GM Directives pacing matrix, evolution checks, companion recruitment) reference it.

---

## Section Standards

### Special

**Optional — omit this entire section for standard D&D campaigns.** The existing document system — world-info (setting), npc-directory (characters), inventory (items and economy), gm-canon (story threads), companion-guides (companions), gm-directives (GM style) — fully covers standard campaigns.

Include this section only when the campaign meaningfully deviates from traditional D&D adventure structure in ways not captured by other documents. This is for structural or mechanical departures that don't belong anywhere else:

- **Non-standard session structure** — when the campaign follows a fixed schedule rather than open exploration. Example: a tournament arc with a strict match calendar (group stage on days 3/6/9, knockout rounds after, finals as the climax) — the GM needs this to pace sessions and prep correctly.
- **Custom economy or reward systems** — when the standard loot-and-shop model doesn't apply. Example: a tournament setting where there's no looting defeated enemies, so gold comes from match purses scaled by stage (100gp group stage → 10,000gp finals) and item rewards are granted per victory — the GM needs this to run rewards without the usual dungeon economy.
- **Distinct gameplay phases** — when sessions alternate between fundamentally different play modes. Example: off-field preparation phases (scouting rivals, countering sabotage, social maneuvering) followed by structured arena combat — the GM needs to know these phases exist and how they connect.
- **Mechanic-heavy subsystems** — custom systems central enough that every session references them. Example: a tournament store with rotating stock and staged availability, faction reputation tracks, or survival resource management.

If the campaign is a traditional D&D adventure — explore, fight, loot, progress — this section is not needed.

## Template

```markdown
# Campaign Pitch

## General Setting & Tone

* **Core Genre/Setting:** [The campaign's genre. Examples: Classic High Fantasy, Gritty Low-Magic, Urban Intrigue, Cosmic Horror.]
* **Story Tone:** [The desired tone and mood. Examples: Epic and heroic, dark and morally grey, lighthearted comedy, mysterious and suspenseful. Can mix — e.g. "heroic with moments of comedy but also heavy dark themes."]
* **Pillars of Play:** [The player's preferred balance between Combat, Exploration, and Social Interaction. Can specify emphasis — e.g. "Balanced mix. Emphasis on Combat and Exploration."]
* **Inspirations:** [Media references (books, movies, games) that capture the feel.]

## Story and Pacing

* **Campaign Length:** [One-Shot (3-5 sessions) / Short (8-12 sessions) / Medium (15-25 sessions) / Long (30+ sessions)]
* **Story Scope:** [The desired narrative scale. Examples: Local heroes saving a town, national-level political conflict, world-saving epic quest. Can escalate — e.g. "Starts small and local to gradually grow into larger stakes."]
* **Story Pacing:** [How quickly the core arc surfaces and drives play. Examples: "Slow burn — start local and self-contained, core arc stays distant for several sessions before gradually surfacing" / "Steady build — core arc is present from the start, escalates gradually" / "Immediate — core arc is the content from session one"]
* **Core Arc:** [The campaign's central storyline or conflict. Can be defined at creation from character-info or set to "Undefined — let it emerge through play." Updated via evolutions system when an arc crystallizes or resolves.]
* **Secondary arcs:** [Optional. Additional story threads from the PC's backstory or world that fall outside the Core Arc — unresolved backstory threads (fate of a hometown, a missing mentor), faction conflicts, regional tensions. Companion personal growth arcs are NOT secondary arcs — they are tracked separately as companion threads in gm-canon. Secondary arcs have more pacing freedom than the Core Arc.]

## Party Preferences

* **Party Composition:** [Solo adventure or a party with companions. If companions, how many. Example: "A party with two companions."]
* **Companions Pitch:** [Any preferences for companion traits, races, classes, or personalities. Can be specific or open-ended. Omit if no companions.]
* **Companion Recruitment:** [How companions join: "Organic recruitment" through gameplay or "Present from the start" as part of the opening scenario.]

## Special

[Campaign-specific mechanics, structures, or rules that meaningfully deviate from traditional D&D adventure structure. See Section Standards above for guidance on when to include this section and what belongs here.]
```
