# Character Info — Writing Standards & Template

Writing standards for character-info documents. This document captures a player character's identity, backstory, and narrative anchors. It is the primary source the GM uses to understand the character's motivations and emotional landscape.

character-info is player-authored content — the AI assists in organizing and drafting but the player's creative vision drives the character. Unlike companion guides (which are GM tools), this document reflects what the player wants their character to be.

---

## Section Standards

### Core Information

* One-liner fields: Name, Role (always "Player Character (PC)"), Race, Gender, Age, Languages, Alignment.
* Age can be a number or a range (e.g., "Young Adult", "Middle-aged", "160").
* **Romance Framework** — optional. Omit by default. Add the line `Romance Framework: Disabled` only when the PC is a child or the player has explicitly opted out of romantic content for this character.

### Description

* **Appearance:** Physical appearance — height, build, hair, notable features, typical attire/armor, weapons carried. Include distinctive visual details that make the character recognizable. 2-4 sentences.
* **Personality:** Core personality traits, flaws, mannerisms, and behavioral tendencies. How they interact with others, what drives them, what they avoid. Should give the GM enough to portray the character accurately in narration. 2-4 sentences.

### Backstory

* A coherent, multi-paragraph narrative of the character's history. Weave together significant events, relationships, and turning points into a single cohesive story. This is the primary source for understanding the character's motivations.
* Target 3-6 paragraphs depending on complexity. Dense and specific — avoid generic fantasy backstory filler.
* All important places, people, and organizations must have proper names — not generic descriptions like "a dwarven forge" or "a coastal city." If the player hasn't named something, propose a name that fits the world's cultural texture.
* Should naturally surface the hooks and tensions that drive the character forward.

### Key Figures Or Items (Anchors)

* The most important people, items, and entities from the character's backstory. Each gets its own sub-header.
* These are the narrative anchors the GM uses to create meaningful story connections and callbacks.
* Each entry has:
  * **Tagline:** A short, evocative subtitle summarizing their role (e.g., "The mentor who taught him that building things that protect people is its own kind of devotion.").
  * **Description & Relationship:** One or more paragraphs detailing the entry's significance to the character, their history together, and any plot hooks associated with them.
* Typically 3-5 anchors. Quality over quantity — each should be narratively load-bearing.

## Template

```markdown
# Character Info - [Name]

## Core Information

* **Name:** [Character's full name]
* **Role:** Player Character (PC)
* **Race:** [Character's race]
* **Gender:** [Character's gender]
* **Age:** [Character's age or age range]
* **Languages:** [Languages spoken, e.g., "Common, Infernal"]
* **Alignment:** [Character's alignment]
* **Romance Framework:** Disabled  <!-- omit this line by default; include only for child PCs or explicit player opt-out -->


## Description

* **Appearance:** [Physical appearance — height, build, hair, notable features, typical attire/armor, weapons carried. Include distinctive visual details.]
* **Personality:** [Core personality traits, flaws, mannerisms, and behavioral tendencies.]

## Backstory

[Coherent, multi-paragraph narrative of the character's history.]

## Key Figures Or Items (Anchors)

#### [Name or Title]
* **Tagline:** [A short, evocative subtitle summarizing their role.]
* **Description & Relationship:** [Significance to the character, history together, plot hooks.]
```
